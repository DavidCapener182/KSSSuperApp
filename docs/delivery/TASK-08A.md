## Purpose and boundary

Keep the existing Event chain **Client → Site → Event → Staffing Requirement → Allocation**. Add a distinct operational chain **Client → Site → Site Service → dated Shift Demand → Allocation** for warehouse, gatehouse, retail/static, office/site and recurring day/night security cover. A Site Service is the ongoing identity of the service delivered at one exact Client-linked Site. It is neither an Event, an individual shift nor a contract. Reuse `crm_organisations.id`, `sites.id`, the historical `site_client_links` relation, `operational_roles.id`, `people.id`, existing availability and allocation principles, and the single Workforce weekly view. Do not create duplicate Clients, Sites, People, roles or a second rota.

Synthetic development only. No real client details, commercial values, MagSecure, patrol execution, attendance, worked time, payroll, invoicing, automated scheduling or production eligibility policy. The existing synthetic SIA → `SECURITY_GUARDING` rule is **DEV ONLY**; it must never be described or migrated as live policy.

## Site Service identity and authority

Propose `site_services` with stable UUID `id`, immutable `site_id` and `site_client_link_id`, `name`, controlled `type`, `state`, `effective_from` and nullable `effective_until` London dates, `owner_person_id`, created/updated actor and timestamps, and optimistic `revision`. Client is derived from the exact historical Site-Client link; it is not an independently editable Client field. Creation requires an ACTIVE Site, a current Client link, and a Client Organisation at that link. If the Site-Client link later changes, do not silently reparent a Service: end or explicitly reconcile the old Service and create a new identity under the new link in a separately approved correction path. Safe display may include the derived Client and Site labels, never CRM pipeline or Contact private details.

Initial `type` enum: `STATIC_GUARDING`, `GATEHOUSE`, `RETAIL_SECURITY`, `PATROL`, `RECEPTION_SECURITY`, `OTHER`. `PATROL` classifies a possible service, but adds no checkpoint, route or MagSecure integration. Keep free-text name for the business label; the enum is deliberately small. Do not store contract value, charge rate, pay rate or personnel evidence on Service.

Lifecycle is guarded and historically attributed:

| State | Exact meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `DRAFT` | Setup only. Templates may be prepared; no current dated demand is generated and no allocations are allowed. |
| `ACTIVE` | Within effective dates, approved template versions may produce dated demand; dated demand may be staffed. This says nothing about contract status or readiness. |
| `PAUSED` | No new ordinary recurrence occurrences are generated for the pause interval. Existing dated shifts and allocations remain visible and require explicit reconciliation; pause never cancels them. Resume is a reasoned transition with an effective date. |
| `ENDED` | Terminal for future generation and new allocation. Historical dated shifts, allocations, responses and audit stay intact. Ending requires an explicit disposition of future dated demand and active allocations; it does not delete or silently cancel them. A new service period after end needs a new Service or separately approved correction policy. |

Effective dates and pause intervals are London **service/report dates**, with an exclusive end boundary in storage. State changes require actor, timestamp, reason and effective date. Backdating cannot rewrite occurred demand or attendance. Office Admin and Super Admin create/manage Service and recurring templates because those actions define continuing Client/Site scope. Operations has bounded authority over **dated demand, exceptions and staffing** within an active Service at an authorised operational Site, with reasoned actions and exact IDs. Operations may not edit the ongoing template, manage Site-Client links/SiteAssignments, view CRM pipeline or private Contacts/People, or access Staff Profile, Documents, onboarding evidence or HR. SiteAssignment alone grants no Service or staffing management authority. Super Admin oversight is audited and still follows exact record/action policy.

## Weekly recurrence and stable dated demand

Use a bounded **weekly template**, not a general calendar engine. Each Service can have multiple template lines, each with a stable line UUID, selected weekdays (1–7, Europe/London report date), one existing operational role ID, positive required quantity, report local time, shift start/end local times, area and reporting point, effective-from/until dates and revision. End on the next local day when the configured end is at or before the start; report time may precede shift start but must be within a bounded permitted lead. Store a clear overnight flag/day offset, validate an exact UTC interval for each generated date, and apply existing duty/lead technical bounds where appropriate. DST gaps/ambiguities require an explicit deterministic policy and test; never silently move a duty to a different wall time. Operational role rules remain source policy, not a qualification assertion.

Choose **materialised, bounded future dated demand** rather than resolving mutable templates at read time. A dated row has a stable UUID before allocation, exception, amendment, cancellation, attendance or timesheet can refer to it. Generate a rolling horizon, proposed 8 weeks, plus explicit bounded on-demand extension for an authorised planning week. A transactionally guarded, idempotent generator creates a unique `(service_id, template_line_id, service_date)` occurrence for a published template version; generation uses an exact version snapshot and never updates an existing dated row as a side effect of a later template edit. On template publication, compare already materialised future occurrences: preserve each existing ID and version provenance, explicitly supersede/revise only unallocated future rows with typed history, and block allocated rows pending manager reconciliation. The unique line/date key prevents a second active occurrence from silently replacing the first. Reconcile the horizon daily or on publication; do not generate unbounded history/future rows. Surface generation failures and missing horizon coverage rather than implying a complete schedule. Read-only Workforce counts only persisted dated demand, with horizon status shown to managers where relevant.

A dated `site_shift_demands` row contains `id`, Service and template line/version provenance, London `service_date`, existing role ID, required quantity, exact `report_at`, `shift_starts_at`, `shift_ends_at` UTC instants, area/reporting point, `PLANNED` or `CANCELLED`, revision and attribution. A typed manual extra shift may have no template line but must still reference the Service and an exception record. Required, Allocated, Remaining and Accepted derive from the dated row and active allocations; no cached readiness or generic availability flag. A day/night pair remains two exact rows on the report date; the overnight row is displayed once, with next-day finish labelled.

The template is intent, the dated row is operational demand, allocation is a named Person commitment, Staff `ACCEPTED` is a response, availability is a separate declaration, and attendance/worked hours are later facts. None of these imply the next.

## Exceptions, versioning and reconciliation

Publish immutable template versions with an effective date range. To change night quantity from 1 through 31 October to 2 from 1 November, close the old version at the exclusive 1 November boundary and publish a new version from 1 November. Keep line identity where it represents the same duty, but bind each occurrence to its exact version. Store typed immutable `site_service_events`, `site_shift_template_events` and `site_shift_demand_events`, including prior/new values or exact version references, actor, time, reason and source action. Generic audit events supplement these business histories; neither may be rewritten by ordinary users.

Dated overrides are explicit records keyed to Service, date and target occurrence (or new extra occurrence): `SKIP`, `ADD`, `CHANGE_TIME`, `CHANGE_QUANTITY`, `CHANGE_REPORT_POINT` and `CANCEL`, with reason, actor and revision. Examples include bank holiday coverage, an extra guard, skipped night, temporary cover and a changed report time. Resolve a date by publishing an override onto its stable dated row or a new extra row; never mutate a past template to represent an exception. A replacement/changed duty retains its row ID only when the exact operational context can be safely amended under the allocation guard; otherwise cancel with reconciliation and create a linked replacement row. Historical versions and overrides remain reconstructable even after subsequent changes.

No template edit, pause, end, generation run or exception may silently move/remove an `ALLOCATED` or `ACCEPTED` Person. Context changes to role, report/shift time, Site, area or reporting point on a row with active allocations are blocked pending explicit per-allocation reconciliation. Capacity reduction below active count is rejected. Cancellation with active allocations requires an atomic, reasoned action that either cancels each affected allocation with its own typed history and Staff-visible status, or is rejected pending manager action; there is no invisible cascade. Past dated rows and terminal allocation history cannot be deleted. If future attendance later exists, its correction policy will be separately approved.

## Allocation architecture: one conflict authority

The present `event_staff_allocations` schema and `deployment_allocate`/candidate helpers are Event-specific. Propose a **dedicated `site_shift_allocations` table and thin static adapter** for exact Site shift FKs and history, while extracting the Person time-conflict and availability evaluation into one private database authority called by **both** Event and static allocation operations. This avoids a polymorphic FK that cannot enforce an exact target without material redesign of accepted Event tables. It is a proposal, not permission to refactor 06C. If implementation shows the shared Person lock or cross-source checks cannot be introduced without changing Event allocation semantics/API/history, stop at that exact design point and ask David to approve the material redesign before migration. Do not ship two independent clash checkers.

Each static allocation has stable UUID, exact `site_shift_demand_id`, exact `person_id`, status `ALLOCATED`/`ACCEPTED`/`DECLINED`/`CANCELLED`, demand revision at allocation, actor/time, response/cancel attribution and optimistic revision. Active statuses consume one position. Terminal statuses release capacity and cannot reopen; replacements receive new IDs. Staff may accept/decline their own row; managers may allocate/cancel with the existing reason and history semantics. No manager may manufacture acceptance. Strict capacity is enforced under a dated-demand row lock and active count check. A partial unique active Person/demand index prevents duplicate active assignments.

For **every** new allocation or relevant time/context mutation, in one database transaction: lock the target demand row for capacity, lock the shared `people` row for that Person, resolve all active Event and Site shift allocations, and reject any overlap of half-open `[report_at, shift_ends_at)` intervals. `end == next report` is allowed; any positive intersection is blocked. Lock order is deterministic (target then Person, or a documented globally consistent order), and all Event/static writers use it; tests must prove there is no deadlock or race under simultaneous cross-source requests. The Event allocation path must be updated to call this shared authority before static allocation can be enabled. Availability is recalculated at action time for the exact duty across both sources through the same 07A policy: current `UNAVAILABLE` overlap hard blocks; full `AVAILABLE` coverage is a positive signal only; partial or undeclared coverage is a warning/review with the existing acknowledgement and reason rule. A later availability edit does not cancel an accepted allocation; the unified conflict indicator surfaces it on both sources. Candidate listings are hints, never the final decision. Source-specific capacity locks remain separate; Person locking is shared.

Configured qualification checks stay exact role/time checks. A nonconfigured role remains review-required under existing policy, not automatically eligible. The synthetic Dev SIA rule remains Dev only. Manager projections return safe codes and explanations, never credential identifiers, evidence bytes, HR notes or a global eligible flag.

## One Workforce and Staff experience

Extend `/workforce` as one guarded weekly composition of Event requirement rows and persisted Site shift demand rows. Each row has an explicit source discriminator and exact source ID; a Site Service group shows safe Client, Site and Service labels and **Ongoing Site shift**, while Event groups retain **Event work**. The same week/date, Site, safe Client, role, gap and conflict filters apply to both. Required = sum current planned quantity; Allocated = active statuses; Remaining = Required minus Allocated; Accepted = accepted status count. Explicit Unavailable and removed-coverage conflict counts are distinct from partial/not-declared warnings and from staffing gaps. Counts and paginated rows derive from one authorised filtered set and one snapshot. There is no second rota, readiness verdict, compliance claim or inferred attendance. Deep links open the exact source action surface and recheck authority.

Add Site shift rows alongside Event work in existing **My Deployments** and **My Schedule**, labelled **Ongoing Site shift** with safe Client/Site/Service, role, reporting point, report and overnight finish, own response and own availability indicator. Staff use one accept/decline experience and may see only their own allocations; no peer names, full demand or manager counts. Declined/cancelled history remains available through the existing history pattern. Staff can navigate to My Availability without that declaration becoming an assignment. Manager Staff schedule composes both sources under the same narrow operational Person projection.

Desktop uses the existing Workforce week and Staff schedule structure with source chips and compact day groups. At about 390px, use a selected-day view and stacked cards: source label, Service/Event, Site, role, exact overnight time and factual counts remain readable without horizontal overflow. My Schedule uses own-day cards; My Deployments keeps response controls reachable and clearly scoped. Keyboard/focus, touch targets, empty/error states and non-colour status labels follow the current iOS-inspired shadcn/ui blue/graphite/neutral design. **Absolutely no green**, including availability or acceptance states.
