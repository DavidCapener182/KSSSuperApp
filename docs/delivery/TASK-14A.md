# TASK-14A proposal — routine Site occurrence and shift handover book

**Status:** Proposal only, 24 September 2026. No implementation, migration, synthetic or live data write, deployment, or phase advance is authorised by this document. David must approve a bounded implementation separately.

## Purpose and boundary

Give authorised Site Service staff and operational managers a factual, attributable book for routine site activity and shift handover. It records what was observed, received, passed on and acknowledged. It is distinct from the delivered TASK-12A Incident report and from MagSecure patrols: no checkpoint, patrol run, tag scan, compliance claim, incident investigation, emergency dispatch or automatic Incident creation. An urgent event still follows the site's real emergency procedure. The book cannot establish that an instruction was approved by a client, that a contractor was vetted, that equipment was returned, or that a handover receiver acted on an item.

The first slice is synthetic Dev only. One Site Service book serves all day/night and multi-person duties at that Service. Entries can be made with legitimate Site/Service authority even when the author has no allocation. Do not force an Event identity into this Service workflow or create a second rota. Preserve all accepted source identities.

Preserve a context snapshot of safe Service/Site labels only for historical display, never as an access grant.

## Entry and handover data

Use one small controlled `entry_type` list: `VISITOR_CONTRACTOR_DELIVERY`, `KEYS_EQUIPMENT`, `MAINTENANCE`, `CLIENT_INSTRUCTION`, `ROUTINE_OBSERVATION`, `HANDOVER`, `OUTSTANDING_ITEM`. These are display/routing labels, not findings or proof. Avoid subcategory trees. A delivery or visitor note can distinguish arrival/departure in concise neutral text; it is not an identity-check or visitor register. A key/equipment note records a neutral descriptor and reported custody action, without creating an asset ledger. A client instruction records who conveyed it in a minimal role/organisation description and marks it **unverified instruction** until separately confirmed through an approved process; it does not silently amend the Site operational pack. An outstanding item states the action needed and a responsible shift/role, not a private HR assignment. Free text is short and factual, with a maximum length and server validation.

Each accepted entry version has exact `author_person_id` resolved from authenticated identity, `occurred_at` supplied by the author, database `recorded_at`, original local wall time/UTC offset (Europe/London display), text, type, source-of-knowledge (`OBSERVED` / `REPORTED_TO_ME`), and immutable revision/event identity. Reject nonexistent DST times; require a repeated-hour choice. `recorded_at` is when the server accepted the version, not when a device saved a draft. Never backdate the server time. An entry can be late and must visibly show both times. No one can impersonate the original author through a manager entry.

The separate handover record has stable UUID, exact Service, a bounded duty window (`starts_at`, `ends_at`) and named outgoing actor. It may cite exact book entry IDs and unresolved item IDs, with a short summary and next-shift audience. A handover does not require or create a single allocation. Multiple outgoing contributors may add attributable items; multiple incoming staff may acknowledge independently. Show who has acknowledged and who has not, without implying that one acknowledgement covers all people on the next shift. Incoming acknowledgement binds the exact handover revision, receiver Person, server time and displayed item set; a later correction/new item requires a new revision and fresh acknowledgement to claim the revised set was seen. If no incoming person is yet known, show “Awaiting acknowledgement,” not a fabricated recipient. A manager can record a reasoned operational closure of a stale handover, but cannot acknowledge on someone's behalf.

An outstanding item has its own stable UUID and status derived from append-only `OPEN`, `RESOLVED` and reasoned `REOPENED` events. Resolution records actor, time and factual resolution note; it does not erase the original or retroactively change a handover. Open items remain in the next-shift view across day/night boundaries until resolved. Resolved items remain in history. A handover may reference an item by exact ID and observed revision, while the next-shift list reads current status; clearly label “changed since handover” if the status/revision differs. Avoid an unbounded copy of every open item into each handover.

## Authority, privacy and lifecycle

Proposed Staff authority requires an active `SECURITY_STAFF` role plus exact current Site Service duty access: their own active/accepted allocation on a demand for that Service and duty window, **or** a separately granted finite Site Service book contributor permission for legitimate unallocated relief/cover. A generic SiteAssignment, historical allocation, Site name match or Auth account is insufficient. The grant is Person+Service+date range, reasoned, attributable, revocable and Super Admin/authorised Office managed; it does not confer workforce, private personnel or Incident permissions. This explicit unallocated path needs David's approval. Staff may create factual entries, contribute to handovers, acknowledge as self, read the Service's operational book during their permitted window plus a bounded handover lookback, and append reasoned corrections to their own entries. They cannot edit another author's entry, resolve another person's item without approved operational responsibility, or inspect unrelated Services.

Operations may read and action the exact Service book only with active Operations role and the existing operational Service/Site scope plus a proposed explicit `SITE_BOOK_MANAGER` action grant. This grant covers manager entries, corrections with reason, item resolution/reopen, handover oversight and bounded search; it never confers Incident Reviewer rights. Office Admin receives no blanket book detail from CRM or Site creation rights. Super Admin oversight is attributable, with privileged reads and actions audited. Current role, Service scope and any grant must be rechecked at every list, detail, search, history, correction, acknowledgement, resolve and export action. Site Service state changes must not delete history; future writing to an ended Service is denied except a separately authorised, reasoned historical correction. A paused Service may still require handover on existing duty, so apply current duty/context policy rather than treating pause as blanket visibility.

Use server-side action policy and guarded database operations, with RLS and no direct authenticated writes to base/history tables. The scope is Service and action specific; linked Site/demand/allocation never widens the entry audience. Keep Staff-visible text operational and minimal. No personal phone, address, DOB, ID document, SIA, medical, safeguarding, disciplinary, allegation or protected characteristic details in generic entries. Show an inline prompt before typing: “Record only the operational facts needed for the next shift. Do not include private personnel, medical or safeguarding details. Use the approved Incident or restricted process when appropriate.” Client representatives, contractors, visitors and delivery drivers are neutral role/organisation descriptors only, with no new Person or Contact record. No attachments in this slice. Do not replicate text into generic `audit_events`, logs, URLs, notifications, search telemetry or analytics. Business history stores protected text; generic audit stores actor, action, record ID and timestamp only. Privileged reads are attributable without copying content.

Corrections append an immutable version with author, exact prior revision, reason and changed fields. Original versions remain available to the permitted audience and show a clear corrected marker. No hard delete, overwrite, silent retargeting or correction of `recorded_at`. Maintain distinct status/event history for item resolution and handover acknowledgement. Retention duration, lawful basis, controller/processor roles, subject rights, legal hold, archival/deletion mechanism and any client access are pre-live decisions. Do not load real records before these are agreed. Search is bounded by exact authorised Services, date, type and open status, with server filtering before pagination/counts; avoid full-text indexing of names or sensitive narrative until privacy review. No client portal, broad export or aggregate analytics in the first slice.

## API, concurrency and offline state

Propose guarded operations `site_book_submit`, `site_book_correct`, `site_book_list/detail`, `site_book_open_item`, `site_book_resolve/reopen_item`, `site_handover_submit`, `site_handover_acknowledge` and a bounded next-shift projection. Names/signatures are candidates to verify against current functions before implementation. Every mutation derives actor from Auth, checks exact Service/action context inside the transaction, applies optimistic expected revision and appends business event plus safe audit atomically. A per-actor, per-action idempotency UUID and canonical request hash makes a retry return the accepted result; changed-payload key reuse fails. Lock the entry/item/handover row for revision transitions. A concurrent correction versus acknowledgement either binds the old exact revision with a visible superseded state or conflicts cleanly; it never silently claims the receiver saw later text. Duplicate acknowledgement by the same Person/revision returns the prior result; a second receiver gets a distinct row. Direct database writes, delete, history rewrites, spoofed actor and cross-Service IDs are denied.

Online server acceptance is the only “Recorded” state. At 390px, an unsent form may remain in memory while the page is open, labelled “Not sent”; do not persist sensitive drafts in localStorage, IndexedDB, service workers or an offline queue. If connection drops, show “Not sent — retry when online” and preserve the in-memory draft while the page remains open. After an ambiguous timeout, use only the author's scoped idempotency-result lookup before retry. A local draft or queued network request is never shown as received. Warn on deliberate navigation that unsent text may be lost without leaking content.
